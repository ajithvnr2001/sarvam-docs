## Answer a customer's questions  
before they book

Customers often ask about location, cost, preparation, or who they will see before they are ready to book. Connect the agent to your knowledge base and it answers in the same call. It stays within your approved information, and transfers the call when a person needs to take over.

## Built around how  
people actually talk

Customers describe what they need in their own words, and the agent works out the rest.

Natural language

### No exact names needed

Loose descriptions and incomplete names are enough. When two branches or specialists sound alike, the agent asks which one.

Flexible scheduling

### Real dates, spoken naturally

“Tomorrow evening” and “the Friday after next” become real slots. Customers can interrupt or change their mind mid-call.

Multilingual conversations

### Multiple bookings, one call

Customers can start a sentence in Hindi and finish it in English. One call can cover two bookings, for example two family members.

## Connected to your systems

Connect the tools you already use to manage appointments. The agent reads live availability, completes the action in your system of record, and confirms the outcome in the same call.

### Availability

Your scheduling software shows which slots are open. Staff and resource calendars show which people and resources are free for each one.

### CRM and catalogues

Your CRM and customer records identify the caller. Service catalogues tell the agent what you offer and branch and location systems tell it where.

### Payments and handoff

Payment systems handle charges tied to the booking. SMS, WhatsApp, and email send confirmations, and handoff systems bring in a person when needed.
